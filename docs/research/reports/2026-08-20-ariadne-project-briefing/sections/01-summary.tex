\section{Summary}

\begin{rrsummary}
Ariadne answers security-analyst questions in English by querying two graph
databases in their own query languages. Nineteen recorded experiments asked one
underlying question: are its hand-written query templates worth their cost?

\textbf{One result held up.} Templates beat letting a language model write the
database query. On two question sets that share no entity, the template cascade
scored 0.9467 and 0.9417. An arm in which the model writes the Cypher or Gremlin
itself scored 0.5000 and 0.4183 (Fig.~\ref{fig:two-banks}, $n = 30$ per cell).
Quadrupling that arm's token budget moved it to 0.5367.

\textbf{Most other headline numbers did not hold up.} The near-perfect 0.9972 on
the 120-question development catalogue fell to 0.689 on 30 questions written
without sight of the code. Two software defects and one flaw in the question set
account for the fall. Repaired, those same 30 questions score 0.9350
(Fig.~\ref{fig:six-numbers}).

\textbf{The claim that templates hold the accuracy failed outright.} An arm with
every template disabled scored level with or above the cascade on all three
question sets. What separates the two is refusal quality: the cascade shows the
query behind a refusal on 20 of 21 catalogue refusals, against 11 of 20
(Table~\ref{tab:abstention}).

\textbf{Read the caveats.} No result here is measured against a system outside
this repository. The merged-graph comparison, which the funding pitch rests on,
has never been built.
\end{rrsummary}

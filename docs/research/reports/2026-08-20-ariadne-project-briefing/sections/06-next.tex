\section{What's next}

\subsection{One decision is blocking}

The merged-graph comparison cannot proceed until someone chooses between two
repairs to the Neo4j loader, and the choice is a design judgment rather than a
bug fix.

\textbf{Option A} widens the relationship-name rule to match the label rule
already beside it. One character class. The merged graph then names
relationships exactly as the CAPEC source does.

\textbf{Option B} renames CAPEC's six mixed-case relationships to the loader's
existing convention. The safety rule stays untouched. The merged store then
names its relationships differently from the Gremlin store holding the same
data, so a query written against one does not port to the other.

Option B quietly handicaps the merged arm in the comparison it exists to inform.
Option A touches a function named for safety. Whichever is chosen, the merged
store must be wiped and reloaded from empty, since it currently holds half a
catalogue.

\subsection{Recommendations this report supports now}

\begin{description}
  \item[Stop quoting a single arm ranking.] The top three configurations are
    inside the prose-writing arm's own run-to-run variation, and their order
    swapped between the two generated sets. Report them as not separable, or run
    each arm three times and report the spread.
  \item[Re-grade with a capable independent model.] Do this before anything
    leaves the repository. The second grader in Table~\ref{tab:cross-grader}
    emitted two distinct scores and cannot resolve this range. The earlier
    Sonnet~5 panel can. Roughly one grading round.
  \item[Record the second night in the research record.] The repeat runs, the
    second question set, the token-budget re-run and the cross-grading exist
    only as a sweep output directory. By this project's own rules they do not
    count until they are scaffolded as an experiment.
  \item[Make question identifiers carry their question set.] The two generated
    sets reuse one identifier scheme over different entities. Nothing is wrong
    today, since every roll-up scopes by directory. It has already produced one
    false diagnosis in a report.
\end{description}

\subsection{Open questions this report did not settle}

\begin{description}
  \item[Where is the coverage cliff?] Every question in every set names an
    entity. Nothing measures the regime where a template router is weakest, so
    the design's price on open-ended questions is unreported. Roughly 40
    non-anchored questions would price it.
  \item[Does the verifier do anything?] No experiment disables the verification
    gates one at a time. Fabrication counts sit near zero across arms, and no
    measurement attributes that to the verifier rather than to the templates.
  \item[How does either grader compare with a human?] A reviewer will ask for an
    agreement statistic on a sample of answers. There is none, and the published
    critique of judge protocols makes this the first question a reader will
    reach for.
  \item[Should the evidence reader be decoupled from the templates?] This is
    the one ambiguity inside the headline result. Either the coupling is an
    artifact of our harness, and the query-writing arm should be measured
    without it. Or guaranteeing consumable output is a genuine property of
    templates, and that arm should wear the cost. This report gives both
    numbers. A paper has to pick one reading and defend it.
\end{description}

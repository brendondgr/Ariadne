\section{What we did}

\subsection{Design}

over one question set, graded by one grader. Arms differ in three places and are
otherwise identical. Table~\ref{tab:arms} lists them.

The query-writing arm is built to be fair rather than easy to beat. The model
receives the real database schema, the resolved seed identifiers, the
constraints the safety gate enforces, and the output-column convention the
harness expects. A rejected query comes back with the rejection reason attached
for another attempt. The model is denied the templates and nothing else.

\subsection{Procedure}

Every arm runs through one entry point. That entry point saves the answer to
each question, the full retrieved evidence, and every executed query:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
uv run python -m utils.evaluation.experiment\_untruncated --arm ARM --root DIR
\end{quote}

Grading runs separately over those saved files
\source{utils/evaluation/judge_answers.py}. Question sets are generated by
\pathname{utils/specialists/evalset/make_bank.py}. The two multi-phase overnight
rounds are driven by \pathname{scripts/measure_arms.py} and
\pathname{scripts/night2_sweep.py}, which write a phase-by-phase state file.

\subsection{Measures}

\begin{description}
  \item[Correctness] The grader's score from 0 to 1 for one answer against the
    computed expected answer. Reported as an unweighted mean over a set, with a
    95\% confidence interval over questions. This is the primary measure.
  \item[Verdict] The grader's categorical call: \textsc{correct},
    \textsc{partial} or \textsc{incorrect}.
  \item[Answers with a fabrication] The count of answers whose judgment lists at
    least one statement the grader could not find in the retrieved evidence.
  \item[Gold in evidence] The fraction of expected identifiers present in the
    retrieved records. This measures retrieval alone and ignores the answer.
  \item[Refusal with proof] Of the answers that declined to assert, the share
    that attach the executed query as justification for declining.
  \item[Wall clock] Seconds to complete a whole question set, one machine, one
    run, no parallelism across sub-questions.
\end{description}

\subsection{Analysis}

Two graders appear in the record. The July study and holdout v1 use one
independent Sonnet~5 agent per answer, given the expected assertions and the
full retrieved evidence. The August rounds use a locally served model
\source{relay/skynet@qwen38-27B-awq} over the same protocol, calibrated against
the Sonnet judgments on 30 answers at Pearson $r = 0.9275$, running 0.031
generous on the mean.

Grading the same fixed set of deterministic answers three times gives a spread
of 0.0050. That is the noise floor every difference in the August rounds is read
against.

Where a comparison holds the question set constant, it is paired question by
question. The answer-writing repair carries a paired percentile bootstrap, 2000
resamples, seed 1729. Differences between configurations are reported treated
minus reference, so both effects in the July study are negative and directly
comparable.

\subsection{Why this design}

Judge-scored correctness is primary because the alternatives are
self-consistency checks. Three of the four originally specified end-to-end
metrics measure whether the system's output agrees with its own retrieved
records. A confidently wrong pipeline passes all three. All four read 1.0 while
an independent panel scored the same answers at 0.8946.

Grading is cross-model by construction. The first end-to-end evaluation had one
model grading its own output and reported faithfulness 0.973 against an
independent 0.956, which is the self-preference the concern predicted.

Expected answers are computed from raw source files rather than annotated. An
audit of 16 knowledge-graph question-answering datasets found average factual
correctness of 57\% \citep{kgqagen}. Computing gold is a direct response to that
finding, and it is why a question whose gold cannot be computed is dropped.

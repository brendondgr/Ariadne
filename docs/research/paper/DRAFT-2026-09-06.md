## Abstract

Question-answering systems over knowledge graphs almost universally place a
language model between the question and the database: the model writes a query,
the query runs, and the answer is composed from what comes back. That design
makes a large class of comparisons impossible to run cleanly, because the
stochastic query-writing step sits between every variable of interest and every
score.

We describe and measure a system that removes it. Ariadne answers
natural-language security questions over two live knowledge graphs that are never
merged and that speak different query languages — MITRE ATT&CK in Neo4j (Cypher)
and MITRE CAPEC in Apache TinkerPop (Gremlin) — and on every shipped path **no
language model writes a database query.** We use the resulting determinism to run
four controlled comparisons, three of which return null or negative results:

1. **Hand-written query templates do not hold the system's accuracy.** A
   schema-derived path with no templates matches them on the catalogue they were
   written for and beats them by **+0.1684** judged correctness on 288 held-out
   questions (`EXP-2026-09-007`). What templates hold is precision on a familiar
   schema and the ability to decline with a proof; what they cost is coverage,
   and the cost is **−0.6121** retrieval on a schema nobody wrote them for
   (`EXP-2026-08-015`).
2. **But do not therefore generate the queries.** A model given the real schema,
   resolved seeds, the gate's constraints and a repair loop scores **0.4031**
   against the schema-derived path's **0.6546** on the same held-out questions.
   The choice is not templates versus models; it is *authored* versus *derived*
   structure, with generation losing to both.
3. **Federating buys no accuracy over merging.** Merged minus federated is
   **−0.0334** and **+0.0000** on two blind sets with one variable changed and no
   model in either arm (`EXP-2026-08-009`). What survives the null is structural
   and countable: building the merged store required rewriting **1,083 of 9,138
   nodes**; federation rewrote none (`EXP-2026-08-017`).
4. **Benchmarks written by a system's own authors overstate it**, and the
   overstatement's dominant form is not a wrong answer but a question the bank
   does not contain (`EXP-2026-08-007`, `EXP-2026-09-006`).

We also report a methodological result that emerged from running the above and
that we believe generalises past this system: **grading models have a resolution
floor, and near the ceiling most of them cannot see the differences papers
report.** Of three local graders tested on answers already near 1.0, one emitted
a single distinct score, one emitted two, and one of those two **inverted** the
ordering of mediocre and good answers in exactly the band where all three of our
arms sit (`EXP-2026-09-008`). Three of our own claim-level near-misses trace to
this, and one tie-handling defect was a single commit from publishing four false
result reversals.

Finally, we report what the design costs. Exact-record identity across five
paraphrase tiers falls **1.0000 → 0.4667 → 0.4250 → 0.0990 → 0.0000**
(`EXP-2026-08-020`). The system is precise inside its own vocabulary and
worthless outside it, and we would rather report that than a victory.

## 1. Introduction

*(Framing fixed by `D-027` and the 2026-08-26 novelty audit.)*

The architecture in this paper is not the contribution, and saying so early is
what makes the rest of it defensible. Mediator–wrapper federation is
twenty-five years old. "Templates rather than free generation" is a named family
in knowledge-base question answering since 2015. Attributing a failure to its
earliest failing stage is published prior art, as is read-only enforcement on
generated graph queries. A reviewer who suspects we are claiming any of these is
right to reject.

**What is unoccupied is the measurement.** Consider the question *"do
hand-written query templates actually carry a graph-QA system's accuracy?"* To
answer it you need two systems identical everywhere except in how the query is
produced. In an architecture where a model writes the query, you cannot build the
second one: removing the templates removes the thing you were measuring against,
and leaving the model in leaves a stochastic step between the manipulation and
the score. The comparison is not hard, it is ill-posed.

A system that generates no query with a model is therefore a different
*instrument*, not merely a different implementation. That is the claim this paper
makes, and the four comparisons in §5–§7 and §9 are what it buys.

**Contributions.**

- A federated graph-QA system that dispatches native queries in two graph query
  languages to two live, independently governed stores, with no model-written
  query on any shipped path (§3).
- Four controlled comparisons that architecture makes possible, three returning
  null or negative results (§5, §6, §7, §9).
- A calibration method for LLM graders, and the finding that most local graders
  cannot resolve the effect sizes the field reports near the ceiling (§8).
- An honest accounting of what the design costs off its own vocabulary (§10),
  and of two scoring faults we deliberately did not fix (§11).

We claim priority on implementing and measuring. Nothing more.

## 3. System

Five stages: **plan → route → retrieve → verify → compose.**

A planner decomposes a natural-language question into sub-questions and assigns
each to a source. Per-source specialists emit a **native query in that source's
own language** — Cypher against Neo4j for ATT&CK, Gremlin against TinkerPop for
CAPEC. Results are joined on shared identifiers across a declared bridge. A
verifier admits or rejects each candidate claim against the retrieved records. A
composer writes the answer.

**Where the model seams are, stated plainly.** A language model may plan; it may
grade, in evaluation only; and it may compose prose, though the shipped default
composes deterministically. **A language model never writes a query that reaches
a store.** The `llm_query` arm in §6 exists solely as a comparator and is not a
shipped path.

The verifier's four claim gates were ablated individually over 888 candidate
claims. **Disabling any single gate admitted zero additional unsupported claims**
(`EXP-2026-08-014`), which is a weaker result than it sounds: the gates are
redundant with one another on this population, not individually load-bearing.

The two stores hold ATT&CK and CAPEC — the CAPEC side is 6,374 nodes and 8,582
relationships as loaded, across 25 node types and 34 edge types
(`EXP-2026-09-001`).

## 4. Experimental design

**Arms.** Three, differing only in query production:

| Arm | Query produced by |
|---|---|
| `deterministic` | Hand-written parameterised templates |
| `generic` | Assembly from the graph schema at runtime; no templates |
| `llm_query` | A language model, given schema, resolved seeds, gate constraints and a repair loop |

**Question populations.** Nine, of which four carry the weight here: a
development catalogue; an expansion bank built from a schema census so that
coverage is structural rather than convenient (572 questions,
`EXP-2026-09-001`); a 288-question holdout split off and sealed before any repair
was written; and **182 questions from two published external question sets, used
verbatim, neither authored with sight of this code** (`EXP-2026-08-010`).

> That external set is the project's only outside anchor and it appears in none
> of the project's own summaries. §9 uses it; the abstract should too.

**Grading.** An LLM grader scores each answer against gold records. Every judged
figure in this paper was produced under **two named grader families**, and every
grader is named by exact model id, quantisation and date — never by relay alias.
This is not fastidiousness: one alias in this project served three different
models over its lifetime, and a figure attributed to the alias is not attributable
at all.

**Statistics.** Paired percentile bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, seed 1729.

**A ceiling we deliberately did not fix.** Two scoring faults remain in place —
abstention boilerplate that credits a refusal, and a scorer that recognises the
source's identifier vocabulary rather than the graph's (measured at 0.185 on the
subset where it was quantified). **Both inflate scores.** Fixing either partway
through a comparison would have broken the comparison, so both are priced into
every figure and reported in §11.

## 5. Templates versus schema-derived construction

**The primary result, and it replaces a claim this project's own experiments
refuted.**

On the catalogue the templates were written for, the two paths are
indistinguishable. Over ten anchor-disjoint banks, templates score
**0.99 ± 0.0225** and the schema-derived path **1.00 ± 0.0000**, a paired
difference of **−0.01 ± 0.0225** (`EXP-2026-08-015`). Templates do not hold the
accuracy. On their home turf they merely match.

Move to a schema nobody wrote templates for and the ordering does not narrow, it
inverts. On the same ten banks over an identity schema: templates
**0.2392 ± 0.0674**, schema-derived **0.8514 ± 0.0890**, paired difference
**−0.6121 ± 0.0981** (`EXP-2026-08-015`). On a blind set over that schema,
retrieval is **0.1964** for templates against **0.7857** derived
(`EXP-2026-08-011`).

**The failure mode matters more than the gap.** On that foreign schema the
template arm did not decline. It asserted: unsupported-claim rate **0.8214**
against the derived path's **0.1071** (`EXP-2026-08-011`). A template that does
not match the schema still fires, still retrieves something, and still produces
a confident answer about it.

On unseen questions at scale the gap persists and is not an artifact of a
particular bank. Judged correctness, schema-derived minus templates:
**+0.1642** on the 284-question expansion bank (`EXP-2026-09-002`), **+0.1623**
after the template arm's retrieval defects were repaired (`EXP-2026-09-005`), and
**+0.1684** on the 288-question holdout (`EXP-2026-09-007`).

**The repair is the sharpest single piece of evidence in the paper.** We
diagnosed and fixed two template retrieval defects. Template retrieval rose by
**+0.0684**; template judged correctness moved **+0.0012** (`EXP-2026-09-005`).
Retrieving the right records did not make the answers right. Whatever the
templates were costing, it was not recall.

> ⚠️ **Reconciliation needed before submission.** The census-weighted repair gain
> is **+0.0017** against **+0.0503** pooled (`EXP-2026-09-007`). The pooled
> figure is a draw artifact of a bank that deliberately over-samples rare shapes.
> **Both must be quoted, in that order.** `Q32`.

## 6. Templates versus an LLM writing the queries

**Evidence complete; this is the weakest of the three comparisons and must not
lead the paper.**

Given the real schema, resolved seed entities, the gate's constraints and a
repair loop, a model writing the queries scores **0.4031** judged correctness on
the holdout, against templates at **0.4862** and the schema-derived path at
**0.6546** (`EXP-2026-09-007`). On a fresh bank it scored **0.5000** against
templates at **0.9467** (`EXP-2026-08-008`), and it emitted an empty query on
**153 of 284** expansion-bank questions (`EXP-2026-09-002`).

**Then the honest part.** The templates-over-generation margin is
**+0.0831** on the holdout, and it is *positive under both grader families but
statistically solid under only one*:

| Half | Grader family 1 | Grader family 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Development | +0.1147 [+0.0653, +0.1645] | +0.0696 [−0.0024, +0.1450] |
| Holdout | +0.0822 [+0.0362, +0.1308] | +0.0643 [−0.0080, +0.1333] |

Four measurements, four positive point estimates, two intervals excluding zero
and two not. **We report it as positive under both families and solid under one,
and we do not lead with it.** §8 explains why we are able to say which family to
believe, rather than averaging them and hoping.

## 7. Federated versus merged

**An architectural null, and the structural cost that survives it.**

We built both conditions and changed one variable, with no model in either arm.
Merged minus federated judged correctness: **−0.0334** on one blind bank,
**+0.0000** on a second, mean **−0.0167** (`EXP-2026-08-009`). Federating bought
no accuracy. We state that as a result rather than working around it.

What survives is structural and countable. Constructing the merged store required
rewriting **1,083 of 9,138 nodes** — **22.71%** of the CAPEC side, **11.85%** of
the whole store — including re-prefixing 189 taxonomy entries. Federation
rewrote **zero** (`EXP-2026-08-017`). Merging is not free; it is a data-editing
cost paid once per source and again on every refresh.

> 🛑 **A sentence that cannot be written.** A third graph was loaded unmodified
> beside both catalogues, and it is tempting to say it could not legally have been
> copied into a merged store. **The record forbids that claim**: the contractual
> argument is a motivation, the identity records are synthetic, and what was
> actually demonstrated is narrower — that keeping the sources separate asked that
> graph for nothing. Write the narrow version.

**This section must answer a question a reviewer will ask.** SPLIT-RAG reports a
17.6-Hit-point gap between split and merged conditions; ours is neutral. The most
likely reconciliation is that their ablation varies agent count alongside store
topology, so it is not the same variable — but we have to say that ourselves,
with their numbers in front of us, rather than leaving the contradiction for a
reviewer to notice.

## 8. Grading models have a resolution floor

**New material. Its home in the paper is `[DECISION]` — see the note at the top.**

Every judged number in this literature, ours included, is produced by a language
model scoring another model's answer. The field reports correlation against human
labels and stops there. Correlation is the wrong statistic, and this section is
what convinced us of it.

**Method.** Take answers whose true quality band is known by construction, hold a
grader's prompt and protocol fixed, and ask a narrower question than "does it
correlate": *how many distinct values does this grader emit in this band, and can
it therefore represent the effect size we intend to report?* The acceptance
criterion was pre-registered before any judgment existed: **usable near the
ceiling iff `fraction_at_1 < 0.95` and `distinct_values > 2`.**

**Result**, three local graders, 20 answers in the near-ceiling band
(`EXP-2026-09-008`):

| Grader | Mean | Distinct values | Values emitted | Usable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gemma4-31B-mtp` | 1.0000 | **1** | `{1.0}` | ❌ |
| `gemma-4-26B-it` | 0.9750 | **2** | `{0.5, 1.0}` | ❌ |
| `qwen38-27B-v2` | 0.9800 | 3 | `{0.8, 0.9, 1.0}` | ✅ |

A grader that emits one value in a band reports a contrast of exactly zero in
that band **whatever is true**. This is not noise, and averaging more samples
does not help. It is a floor, and it rises as answers approach the ceiling.

**The second finding is worse and, as far as we can determine, unreported.**
`gemma-4-26B-it` does not merely lose resolution — it **inverts**. Scored across
bands, it puts the *low* band at **0.6435** above the *mid* band at **0.6239**,
when the constructed ordering is mid above low (`EXP-2026-09-008`). That is
precisely the 0.40–0.65 range in which all three of our arms sit on the holdout.
A grader that adds noise is survivable; a grader that reverses the ordering is a
different failure, and it is **invisible to the correlation statistics everyone
reports.** We stopped a planned third-family holdout pass on this evidence, with
zero judgments written.

**What this cost us, stated as a limitation rather than a boast.** Our
co-authorship-bias figure is **0.0322** under the one grader that can resolve it
and **0.0000** under a grader that cannot (`EXP-2026-09-006`). The second number
reads as a refutation and is an instrument limit. Six of twelve cells in the
cross-family re-grade are saturated on the second family. And a tie-handling
defect in the roll-up — excluding pairs tied by the reference grader but not
pairs tied by the candidate — was one commit from publishing **four arm-order
reversals that do not exist.** The correct count is zero.

**What it bought us.** Because the instrument was characterised, we can say which
of §6's two grader families to believe instead of averaging them. That is the
whole argument for doing this.

## 10. The coverage cliff

**What the design costs off its own vocabulary, reported rather than buried.**

Exact-record identity across five paraphrase tiers of the same questions
(`EXP-2026-08-020`):

| Tier | T0 (control) | T1 | T2 | T3 | T4 | T5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Records exact | **1.0000** | 0.4667 | 0.4250 | 0.0990 | **0.0000** | **0.0000** |

The control confirms the harness: on unmodified questions the system is exact.
Two tiers of paraphrase halve it. By T4 it is zero, and excluding catalogue dumps
the recall at T4 is **0.0013**. At T3 the system responds to **52.48%** of
questions with a catalogue dump — retrieving broadly rather than answering.

**And it answers about things that do not exist.** On 30 questions naming
entities with no referent, the deterministic arm answered **4**, with up to 558
genuinely retrieved and genuinely cited records behind them
(`EXP-2026-08-020`). One arm abstained on **93.33%** of them, which is the
existence proof that the behaviour is fixable and not the architecture's fault.

Reporting this trade, rather than a victory, is what inoculates the paper against
its own honest finding — and it is the pilot for the strongest follow-on study
this project has.

## 11. Limitations

Every item here is measured. Stating them is cheaper than being caught.

1. **The templates-over-generation result is grader-dependent** (§6). Solid under
   one family on both halves; not solid under the other on either.
2. **No external system baseline.** Every comparison is between arms of this
   system. We have 182 externally-authored *questions* (`EXP-2026-08-010`) — an
   outside anchor on the inputs, not on the systems. This is the single largest
   gap and we do not minimise it.
3. **No human evaluation.** Every correctness number is a model grading a model.
   §8 is what we did instead, and it is a characterisation of the instrument, not
   a substitute for a human anchor.
4. **The holdout is a random split of one bank we authored.** It controls for
   tuning to particular questions. It does **not** control for the co-authorship
   bias we ourselves measure in §5's companion result.
5. **`n_runs: 1`** on several older experiments, with seeds unrecorded (`D-010`).
   The query-writing arm in particular has never been run three times with
   recorded seeds, and until it has, §6's sign is established on one draw.
6. **Two scoring faults left unfixed by design** (§4). Both inflate scores.
7. **The census-weighted repair gain is +0.0017** against +0.0503 pooled (§5).
   Quote both.
8. **One domain, two schemas, two query languages.** Nothing here is a law about
   query construction until it replicates where we wrote no templates.
9. **Coverage of the sources is partial**: 12 of 34 edge types reached, 0 of 12
   facet properties used at census time (`EXP-2026-09-001`).
10. **A reserved-word Gremlin binding costs the `llm_query` arm evidence on some
    questions.** Pre-existing and priced into the published figures, but whether
    it originates with us or with the model is undiagnosed.

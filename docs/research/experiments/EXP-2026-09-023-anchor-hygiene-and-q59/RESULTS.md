Three things this table establishes before H1 or H2 runs:

  review found 37 of the exposed 41 are cross-source collisions (Phishing and
  Brute Force exist in both catalogues). H2's tie-break exists because of this

## 5. Threats to validity

- Step 0a's prototype list is read from a recorded file; the shipped list is
  computed live against the same canonical snapshot the prototype indexed
  (hashes in `data/POINTERS.md`). CAPEC View and Category hits from the shipped
  index (609 across the bank) have no key in the prototype's identity map and
  are dropped from the shipped list — they are never gold, so this can only
  understate the shipped index.
- Step 0b asks every source, which the shipped pipeline does not yet do; the
  shipped pipeline's *actual* wrong-bind count is the D-035 residual (7 per
  tier at T4/T5), and the difference is routing. H2 removes that routing, which
  is why H1 must land first.
- H1's 1 % threshold was chosen on the exposed bank (ISSUES.md).

## 6. What surprised us

The sealed bank's T6 row. Forty invented anchors, fifteen of them binding a
real node — because "Malware" is a sub-technique's name and any invented
malware question says the word.

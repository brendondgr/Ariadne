## 1. Headline

### Operator decision after round 2 (2026-11-17, D-039): one decision model at a time

Only one decision model will be available at a time, so **every result below that needs both
pplx and d3 is not deployable.** That covers the two-model average and its 0.4131 grounding
gate, and the cross-model shortlists. pplx-decider is the default, and d3-lite the fallback.

**The best single-model setups:**
- **Relation choice:** pplx with readings and two orders averaged (0.7306). The best gate is
  pplx choosing from its own readings shortlist (0.3125). d3 with readings and two orders is
  0.6315, with a 0.222 gate.
- **Grounding:** pplx (0.6952, or a 0.2091 gate with two orders). d3 with two orders gives a

The sections below are kept as measured.

### Round 2 (amendment 2): averaging makes the fast models gates

**Averaging each model over two option orders fixes the order instability that kept fast
models from gating.**
- **D4:** d3 with plain readings, averaged, is a relation-choice **gate**. It covers 0.222
  at 0.0485 wrong, and its flip falls from 0.1336 to 0.0754.
- **D2 + D3:** pplx (0.2091 at 0.0488) and d3 (0.2796 at 0.0455), each averaged, are
  grounding **gates**.

**Averaging the two models adds little on relation choice, but a lot on grounding.**
- **D4:** +0.004 [−0.007, +0.017] over pplx with readings (H8 falsified).
- **D2 + D3:** accuracy rises by +0.040 [+0.015, +0.068], and gate coverage doubles to
  0.4131 at 0.0491 wrong. This was not a pre-registered hypothesis.

**pplx's readings shortlist** keeps 0.95 of gold answers in about 1.4 options. pplx choosing
from it is just outside the non-inferiority margin: −0.017 [−0.040, +0.007] (H9 falsified).
But on that short menu, pplx is a D4 gate covering 0.3125 at 0.0483 wrong (not
pre-registered).

**Still true:** the items a fast gate accepts are ones Qwen already answers the same way
(0.96–1.0). So these gates save calls rather than errors.

### Round 1

**Wording is the one change that works.** Giving each relation option a plain one-line reading
raises pplx-decider's relation-choice (D4) accuracy from 0.5797 to 0.7284: +0.149, 95%
interval [+0.074, +0.224]. It raises d3-lite's by +0.116 [+0.050, +0.179]. Nine tenths of
pplx's gain (0.8985) comes from items whose gold relation shared its description with another
option.

**Nothing else helps on its own, and no change makes a fast model a D4 gate.**
- **Yes/no per option fails.** pplx's yes/no answers barely depend on the option. d3's are
  worse than its forced choice.
- **The rule filter does not help pplx, and hurts d3.** pplx moves +0.029, an interval that
  includes 0. d3 falls by −0.160.
- **The shortlist keeps the gold at 0.95 recall with about two options.** The chooser is no
  worse on it in one cell of eight. It saves calls but adds no accuracy.
- **The verifier is a worse gate than the picker's own confidence**, in every pair.

**Hypotheses:**

| | Verdict |
|---|---|
| H1 | holds |
| H2, H3, H5, H6 | falsified |
| H4 | holds narrowly, in one cell |

**Where a fast model does gate, it gates only what Qwen already gets right.** The D4 items
that the wording-treated models let through are, in 100% (pplx) and 98% (d3) of cases, items
where Qwen with reasoning gave the same answer. The gate therefore saves calls, not errors.

## 4. Interpretation

### The hypotheses, one by one

**H1 (wording) holds.**
- **pplx:** D4 accuracy rises by +0.149 [+0.074, +0.224], above the ≥ 0.05 bound. 0.8985 of
  the gain comes from shared-description items, above the two-thirds bound. The shared-item
  gain (+0.305) is larger than the unique-item gain (+0.086).
- **d3:** the same pattern.
- **The cost:** "none" recall falls. For d3 it falls by −0.111 [−0.192, −0.039], beyond its
  interval. For pplx the fall of −0.071 has an interval that includes 0. A clearer reading of
  every option makes some option look right more often.

**H2 (yes/no) is falsified.**
- **pplx:** "none" recall rises by +0.646, but accuracy on items whose gold is offered falls
  by −0.655, far past the 0.03 bound. That is the "almost always none" threshold of §2.4.
- **d3:** "none" recall does not move (−0.030 on D4 and +0.000 on D2 + D3), and offered
  accuracy falls.

The failure is the models', not the format's: pplx's P(yes) carries almost no information
about the option.

**H3 (a D4 gate in the wording × yes/no grid) is falsified, narrowly.**
- **d3 with wording** meets the gate's coverage and error bounds (0.2198 at 0.049 wrong). It
  fails only on flips: 0.1336 against a bound of 0.10.
- **pplx with wording** flips less (0.0668) but covers only 0.1293.
- **The yes/no cells** cover nothing.

**H4 (shortlist) holds, in one cell of eight.**
- **The recall and size bound:** pplx meets it on both decisions (0.9589 with 2.071 options on
  D4; 0.9508 with 1.242 on D2 + D3). d3 meets it on D2 + D3 (0.9508 with 1.181) but not on D4
  (4.093 options).
- **Non-inferiority (margin 0.02):** one cell meets it. pplx choosing from d3's D2 + D3 list
  is at +0.028 [−0.005, +0.058].
- **The near misses:** pplx choosing from its own D4 list is at +0.011 [−0.026, +0.048], and
  from d3's D4 list at +0.004 [−0.022, +0.033]. Both are near zero, with lower bounds just
  past the margin.
- **What the shortlist buys:** a much shorter menu at no loss to pplx. It does not buy
  accuracy.

**H5 (rule filter) is falsified.**
- **pplx:** on narrowed items it rises by +0.029, an interval that includes 0, against a
  bound of ≥ 0.10.
- **d3:** it falls by −0.160.
- **"None" recall:** it does not fall; it rises for both.

The filter's pre-registered rule also over-matches hyphenated kinds (ISSUES).

**H6 (verifier) is falsified.**
- **D2 + D3:** every verifier gives lower gate coverage than the picker's own confidence
  (−0.17 to −0.29).
- **D4:** the best pair (d3 verifying pplx) reaches 0.0155 coverage, far below a gate's 0.20.

### Round 2's hypotheses

**H7a (order averaging, D4) holds, through d3.**
- **d3 with readings, averaged over two orders,** keeps its coverage (0.222) and error
  (0.0485). Its flip falls to 0.0754, under the 0.10 bound.
- **pplx averaged** still covers only 0.1358.

**H7b (order averaging, D2 + D3) holds, through both models.**
- **pplx** reaches 0.2091 coverage at 0.0488 wrong, with flip 0.0856.
- **d3** reaches 0.2796 at 0.0455, with flip 0.0756.
- **Accuracy** does not change (−0.013 and −0.008, both with intervals that include 0).

Averaging buys order stability, not accuracy.

**H8 (two-model average, D4) is falsified.** It moves +0.004 [−0.007, +0.017] over pplx with
readings. Averaging both models over both orders reaches 0.7457, +0.015 over pplx averaged,
and that interval also includes 0.

**H9 (readings shortlist) is falsified on non-inferiority.**
- **The recall and size bound holds:** 0.9534 recall with 1.403 options.
- **Non-inferiority fails:** pplx on the list is −0.017 [−0.040, +0.007], so the lower end is
  past −0.02.

**Not pre-registered, so to be confirmed before it is relied on:**
- **The two-model average on grounding is the strongest fast gate measured.** It covers
  0.4131 at 0.0491 wrong, with flip 0.063, against 0.2015 for pplx alone, and its accuracy
  rises by +0.040. That is still below Step 7's Qwen-by-probabilities gate (0.5598 at
  0.0437), but it uses no general model.
- **pplx choosing from its own readings shortlist is a D4 gate**: 0.3125 at 0.0483 wrong,
  flip 0.0603. On a menu of one or two options its top probability separates right from
  wrong far better, at a cost of −0.017 accuracy.

### What it means for the agent (Step 8 onward)

**Round 2 adds three things** (the second is superseded by D-039: one decision model only):
- **Always average a fast model over two option orders** before using its confidence as a
  gate. That costs one extra request, of about 0.2–0.3 s.
- ~~**On grounding, average pplx and d3:** it has the largest fast-gate coverage.~~ Not
  deployable under D-039. Use pplx with two orders (0.2091 gate), or d3 with two orders
  (0.2796) where d3 is the model on hand.
- **On relation choice there are two candidate gates.** One is d3 with readings, averaged;
  the other is pplx on its readings shortlist. Each gates about a fifth to a third of items.

Every gate accepts items that Qwen also gets right (0.96–1.0), so each one buys latency and
cost, not accuracy. The items left over still go to the controller.

**From round 1:**

1. **Ship plain readings as relation option text.** This is the one cheap, measured win.
   Step 8's tool catalog generates option text from the card; it should carry one plain
   reading per (relation, direction, target kind), the way `data/readings.json` does.
2. **Do not use either fast model as a yes/no judge or as a verifier.** pplx-decider is not
   built for it (§2.4). d3's yes/no ranks worse than its own forced choice.
3. **A fast model can trim the menu for itself.** pplx's shortlist keeps 0.95 of gold
   answers in about 2 of 13 relations. Use it to shorten what the controller sees, not to
   decide.
4. **Fast-model gates in their current form save calls, not errors.** The D4 items that
   wording lets through are ones Qwen already answers identically (1.0 for pplx, 0.9804 for
   d3). So the value of a gate is latency and cost.
5. **Keep d3 away from short menus.** On one or two real options plus "none", it says "none"
   far too often (§2.3, §2.5).

"None of these" (Q62) remains unsolved by any treatment here. Wording makes it worse, and
the treatments that raise it do so by saying "none" indiscriminately.

## 5. Threats to validity

- **The tuning banks only.** Every figure is on the Sonnet *tuning* banks, as in Step 7. The
  sealed held-out banks are unread. The readings were written once and frozen before any
  call, so they were not tuned on these results. But they were written knowing the relation
  set, and they are relation-specific.
- **A smaller D4 population.** It is 464 items, not Step 7's 483, because this experiment's
  role filter drops items with fewer than two real options. Control accuracy is therefore
  re-computed on 464 (pplx 0.5797 here, against 0.5942 in Step 7). Every difference is paired
  on the same 464.
- **One run each.** Order effects are measured by flips, not by repeated runs.
- **pplx at temperature 1.0**, as in Step 7. Its drift agreement of 1.0 shows the
  probabilities are stable.
- **The rule filter's matching rule** treats a hyphen as a word boundary. "Sub-techniques"
  therefore also counts as naming "technique", so some items were narrowed less than
  intended (ISSUES). This favours the filter if anything, and it still did not help.
- **The yes/no prompt format** was fixed before running and not varied. A different wording
  might move d3, but it cannot fix pplx's position-driven answers (§2.4).
- **End-to-end figures** use Qwen's saved Step 7 answers as the fallback, not a fresh call.

- **Round 2 adds cells.** The two results that were not pre-registered (the D2 + D3
  two-model gate and the D4 shortlist gate) come from a scoreboard with many cells. They
  need confirming on the sealed held-out banks before Step 8 relies on them.
- **Orders 2 and 3 were asked some hours after orders 0 and 1.** The drift re-check (1.0 and
  0.98) shows the servers did not change in between.

## 6. What surprised us

- **Averaging over two orders cuts flips by about a third to a half, at no accuracy cost.**
  The order instability was noise around stable preferences, not a different answer.
- **pplx-decider is not a yes/no model.** Its choice probabilities carry real information,
  with AUROC 0.76–0.81 on its own picks. Asked "yes or no" about one option at a time,
  though, it gives every option about the same answer and follows the answer order. That
  makes "one question per option" unusable with it, whatever the prompt.
- **d3-lite is biased toward "none" on short menus.** Trimming the menu, by rule or by
  shortlist, hurts d3 by about 0.12–0.16. With one real option left, it is right only 39% of
  the time.
- **The gains and the gate are disjoint.** Wording gives a large accuracy gain, and some gate
  coverage. But the gated items are exactly those Qwen already gets right, so the
  end-to-end accuracy is unchanged.
- **d3 with wording misses a D4 gate only on its flip rate (0.13).** That makes order
  stability, not accuracy, the binding constraint for d3. Round 2's averaging removed it.

## Funding

- Seed awards: up to $30,000 per project.
- Number of seed projects: up to 6.
- Follow-on challenge prizes: up to $100,000 for one first-place project and up to $50,000 each for two runner-up projects.
- Follow-on award period: additional 1 year.

## Program Goal

The ReliaQuest Innovation Challenge Fund supports FSU teams developing proof-of-concept solutions in artificial intelligence and cybersecurity. Projects must address one of three corporate security challenge areas and produce a prototype or deployable solution that can be tested during the short award period.

Awardees must present a 5-7 minute pitch at the ReliaQuest Innovation Challenge during FSU Discovery Days in October 2026.

## Challenge Areas

### 1. AI Accuracy Tracking

Develop methods to measure and track AI model accuracy, speed, efficiency degradation, output quality, performance, and cost over time as corporate environments and data sources evolve.

Strong proposals should go beyond monitoring and include recommendations for corrective action, such as improvements to models, prompts, data sets, or other system components.

### 2. Data Sensitivity Classification

Create tools that classify and tag large groups of files by content type and sensitivity level. The goal is to help security teams assess risk and support data loss prevention.

Relevant outputs include classification of general file contents and identification of files containing PII or confidential information.

### 3. Knowledge Graph Integration

Develop methods to search, retrieve, transform, and correlate data across disparate knowledge graphs or graph databases, including systems such as Spanner, Neo4j, or Neptune.

The goal is to reduce the cost and time required to centralize data while still enabling fast synthesis and action across complex information sources.

## Eligibility

- Any full-time FSU faculty, staff, or post-doctoral scholar in any discipline may apply.
- A person may be PI on only one proposal.
- A person may be Co-PI on more than one proposal.
- Single-PI applications are allowed.
- Each proposal must select the best-fitting challenge area at submission.

## Required Application Components

### Portal Text Fields

- PI and Co-PI basic information: name, employee ID, ORCiD, email, requested amount, proposal title, and primary department.
- Non-faculty support, including undergraduate, graduate, and post-doctoral associates.
- Credit distribution by PI and department.
- Keywords, with at least one keyword describing the proposed research.
- Department project team.
- Abstract, maximum 250 words.

### Title Page

- 1 page maximum.
- Must include proposal title and PI/Co-PI names.
- Must be uploaded as a PDF.

### Proposed Research Narrative

- 2 pages maximum, excluding references.
- Appendices are not allowed.
- Format: 1-inch margins, 11-point Times New Roman or Arial.
- Must be uploaded as a PDF.

The narrative should address:

1. Relevance, innovation, and impact.
2. Feasibility and technical merit.
3. Data sources and evaluation strategy.
4. Team and resource readiness.
5. Future development potential and use of possible follow-on funding.

### Curriculum Vitae

- Required for PI and each Co-PI.
- 2 pages maximum per CV.
- Minimum 11-point Times New Roman or Arial.
- 1-inch margins.
- Should include education, relevant positions, research support, and other information relevant to the proposed work.

### Budget

- 1 page maximum.
- Direct costs up to $30,000.
- Should include a table and justification.
- Must be uploaded as a PDF.

Allowable costs:

- Key and other personnel salary, excluding academic-year salary for E&G-funded positions.
- Supplies.
- Equipment, though equipment should be a minor budget component.
- Computer services.
- Contractual services.

Unallowable costs:

- Student tuition.
- Travel.
- Food, meals, drinks, and per diem.

## Review Criteria

Reviewers will evaluate proposals on:

1. Relevance, innovation, and impact.
2. Feasibility and technical merit.
3. Team and resource readiness.
4. Future development potential.
5. Budget justification.

The scoring scale runs from 1 to 9:

- 1: Exceptional, essentially no weaknesses.
- 2: Very good, negligible weaknesses.
- 3: Good, minor weaknesses.
- 4: Satisfactory, numerous minor weaknesses.
- 5: Average, at least one moderate weakness.
- 6: Fair, some moderate weaknesses.
- 7: Marginal, at least one major weakness.
- 8: Poor, several major weaknesses.
- 9: Unacceptable, very few strengths and numerous major weaknesses.

## Strategic Notes for Proposal Development

- The proposal should be written for reviewers from any discipline, with jargon minimized or clearly defined.
- The strongest framing is a narrow, feasible proof of concept rather than a broad research agenda.
- The project should make the business environment use case explicit.
- The technical plan should explain what data will be used, how it will be accessed or generated, and how the prototype will be evaluated.
- Because the award period is short, the work plan should emphasize what can be built and demonstrated by October 2026.
- The proposal should include a credible path from seed prototype to follow-on development if selected for additional challenge funding.
